## Q1 Why PA
I chose PA Consulting because it seems to offer something I have been looking for since studying Electrical and Electronic Engineering: the opportunity to understand a difficult problem and then help turn a solution into something people can actually use. What stands out to me is that PA brings engineers, technologists, designers and consultants together, rather than treating the technical and human sides of a project as separate.

I was particularly interested in PA’s work with the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles to develop a more accessible EV chargepoint concept. My dissertation focused on wireless power transfer for electric vehicles, so seeing how PA combined engineering with research into what drivers and other members of the public actually need made the work feel relevant to me. A solution can be technically impressive, but it has limited value if it is inconvenient or inaccessible.

PA’s values also match how I like to work. “We challenge where it matters” appeals to my curiosity, while “we turn expertise into action” reflects how I approached my university projects, where I preferred testing ideas rather than stopping at theory. Having also worked in a GP surgery and in bioinformatics, I value collaboration across different backgrounds.

## Q2 Project that enthuses you
The project that has excited me most was my final-year dissertation on capacitive wireless power transfer for electric vehicles. I was interested in whether electricity could be transferred efficiently without a physical charging connection and what would need to happen before that technology could be practical.

I set out to reproduce and investigate a published charging-system design. I originally wanted to support the work with physical components, but disruption to material availability meant I had to rely on simulation instead. Rather than simplifying the project, I built a detailed MATLAB model covering the inverter, resonant circuits, coupling plates and rectifier. I used numerical methods to simulate the system at high frequency and compared my results with published findings, achieving around 85% simulated efficiency.

What I enjoyed most was the cycle of researching, testing, getting unexpected results and working out why they happened. I read technical papers, improved my MATLAB skills and learned more about power electronics, resonance, system losses and the practical limitations of wireless charging. The project taught me that a convincing result needs both sound engineering and careful validation.

If I took it further, I would investigate how plate alignment and changing distances affect performance, then build a low-power prototype to compare simulations with real measurements. It has shaped my career ambitions by showing me how much I enjoy solving open-ended technical problems and developing ideas that could make sustainable technology more useful.
